---
title: SCAMPER
level: Intermediate
tags: creativity ideas
description: Systematically vary a product or process along seven questions to generate new ideas.
---

SCAMPER comes from Bob Eberle (1971), who condensed Alex Osborn's checklist into a mnemonic. Each letter stands for one way of changing something that already exists: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse. Instead of waiting for inspiration, you work through the seven questions in order. Many ideas will be useless, but the list makes sure no direction is skipped.

## Example

```prompt
Subject: [e.g. an umbrella]
Audience: [e.g. city commuters]

Apply SCAMPER and give two concrete ideas for each step:
1. Substitute: Which material, part or process could be replaced?
2. Combine: What could it be combined with?
3. Adapt: Which solution from another field could be borrowed?
4. Modify (also magnify or minify): What happens if its shape, size or properties change?
5. Put to another use: What else could it be used for?
6. Eliminate: What could be removed without losing the purpose?
7. Reverse (or rearrange): What happens if you flip the order, direction or structure?

Finally, pick the three ideas with the most value for the audience and briefly justify your choice.
```

## When it helps

Developing products, services and processes further, especially when an idea has stalled. Naming the audience in the prompt keeps the ideas aimed at a real problem. The method fits less well for entirely new fields with no existing starting point, because it always varies something that is already there.
