title: Decomposition

tags: decomposition planning basics
description: Have a large project broken into subtasks with dependencies and a sensible order.
---

Instead of asking for the solution, you ask for the structure: the model breaks a project into subtasks, says which depends on which, and proposes an order from that. This makes a large task plannable and shows early on where the critical points are.

## Example

```prompt
We're a company with [12] employees and want to move our accounting and customer management from [old software] to [new software]. The switch has to be complete by [date] without interrupting day-to-day operations.

Break the project into subtasks. For each subtask, state:
1. What needs to be done, in one or two sentences?
2. Which other subtasks does it depend on?
3. Who should be involved?

Then propose an order. Name the subtasks most likely to delay the schedule, and the questions I need to settle beforehand.
```

## When it helps

Projects, office moves, migrations, longer documents or software work where it is unclear where to start. The dependencies are the most useful part, because they justify the order. Limits: the model does not know your company. The breakdown is a draft that you complete with what you know about systems, contracts and people. Large subtasks can be broken down further with the same prompt.
