---
title: Perspective Taking
level: Intermediate
tags: perspectives analysis
description: Look at a topic from the viewpoint of specific people affected, and ask what would change each one's mind.
---

You have the model present a topic from the viewpoint of different people or groups in turn. The more concretely you describe the people, the less cliché comes out: situation, interests and worries matter more than a label. Asking what would change a perspective's mind shows what its position actually rests on.

## Example

```prompt
Topic: [e.g. introducing a universal basic income]

Present the topic from these three viewpoints:
1. A single father with a part-time job in a small town
2. The managing director of a mid-sized company with 200 employees
3. An economist who researches labor market policy

For each viewpoint:
- Position in two or three sentences, with the main reason
- Biggest hope and biggest worry
- What this person would need in order to change their mind (a figure, an experience, a condition)
```

## When it helps

Contested topics, preparing for conversations with people affected, writing for a mixed audience, finding your own blind spots. Choose perspectives that are genuinely affected in different ways, not three versions of the same view. The model describes perspectives from what it knows; it does not speak for real people. For decisions, it does not replace talking to those affected.
