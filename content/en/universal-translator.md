---
title: Universal Translator
level: Intermediate
tags: translation culture
description: Translate with details about situation and audience so tone and politeness come out right, not just the words.
---

A sentence can be translated in many ways, and which one is right depends on who says it to whom, and in what situation. A translation without context gets the words but often misses the tone. So say who is speaking, who is listening, in what situation and with what intent. Two variants with the difference explained show you the choice the translation makes, and you pick.

## Example

```prompt
Translate the following sentence into German.

Context: I am saying this in a formal meeting to my manager, after a mistake of mine delayed a client project by a week. I want to take responsibility without over-apologizing.

Sentence: "It won't happen again."

Give two variants that differ in tone. For each, explain in one sentence how it will come across to a German-speaking manager and when you would choose it.
```

## When it helps

For anything where tone and relationship matter: emails to managers or clients, apologies, rejections, marketing copy. For languages with formal and informal forms of address, such as German du and Sie, or with several levels of politeness, as in Japanese, context is often decisive. For contracts, medical or legal texts, this does not replace review by a qualified translator.
