---
title: Memetic Proxy
level: Advanced
tags: style role
description: Use a well-known figure or situation as a stand-in that sets style and stance in a few words.
---

Instead of describing a style with a string of adjectives, you name a well-known person, format or situation that embodies it. "Like a teacher in front of a primary school class" or "like a product launch at a keynote" sets tone, pace and attitude toward the audience in a few words. Reynolds and McDonell (2021) described this idea as a memetic proxy: the model knows such situations from its training texts and carries over the behavior that goes with them.

## Example

```prompt
Explain the architecture of this software to me:
[Description or code]

Style: like Steve Jobs presenting a new product. That means plain language, one idea at a time, the benefit to the user rather than technical details, and a closing point that ties everything together.
```

## When it helps

For style, tone and role, when a description would be long and still imprecise. The stand-in has to be well known, to the model and not just to you; with niche figures it guesses. Spell out which traits you mean, or the model will also adopt the ones you do not want, such as pathos or exaggeration. It is no substitute for facts: a famous name does not make an answer reliably more accurate.
