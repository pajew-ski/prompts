---
title: First Principles Thinking
level: Advanced
tags: analysis decomposition
description: Break a problem down to its basic quantities and rebuild the answer from there instead of following comparisons.
---

The idea goes back to Aristotle: first principles are propositions that cannot be derived from anything else. In business, Elon Musk made it popular with batteries. Instead of accepting the market price of battery packs as given, he asked what the materials inside them cost at commodity prices. The gap between that and the market price shows how much of the price comes from manufacturing, margins and habit, and therefore where there is room to move.

The opposite is reasoning by analogy: something costs this much because it costs this much everywhere else.

## Example

```prompt
A battery pack for electric cars currently costs about [price] per kilowatt-hour. I want to understand whether that price is unavoidable.

Do not argue from market comparisons. Work bottom-up:
1. What materials does a battery pack consist of, and in what quantities per kilowatt-hour?
2. What do those materials cost at current commodity prices? State the prices you assume so I can check them.
3. How large is the gap between material cost and market price?
4. What makes up that gap: processing, manufacturing, energy, logistics, margins? Which items are technically necessary, and which result from habit or market structure?

End with the two levers most likely to bring the price down.
```

## When it helps

For costs, prices, processes and assumptions nobody questions anymore. For your own case, swap the battery pack for your product or process. The numbers come from the model and may be outdated or estimated; check them or supply current prices. Not everything reduces to physics: where a question depends on people, law or taste, the "first principles" are themselves assumptions, and the model should label them as such.
