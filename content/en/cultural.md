---
title: Cultural Awareness
level: Intermediate
tags: culture style
description: Adapt a text to a precisely named audience and make the cultural assumptions behind it visible.
---

Without more specific guidance, language models often write from a Western, frequently American perspective. You steer this by naming the audience precisely: country, profession, age, situation. Because any adaptation rests on assumptions, you have the model list them. That way you can check them, since the risk with this technique is stereotypes.

## Example

```prompt
Write a short marketing email for [product, e.g. a time management app].

Audience: [e.g. owners of small trade businesses in Scotland, aged 40 to 60]

Adapt the tone, arguments, examples and form of address to this audience.

Afterwards, list the cultural assumptions you made, for example about formality, values or humor. Mark the ones that rest on thin evidence, so I can check them with someone from the audience.
```

## When it helps

Marketing, localization, internal announcements in international teams, and anywhere the same content has to reach different groups. A precisely described audience leaves less room for clichés: "Scandinavia" invites hygge stereotypes more readily than "procurement staff in Swedish municipalities". The list of assumptions does not replace feedback from people in the audience, but it makes that feedback more targeted.
