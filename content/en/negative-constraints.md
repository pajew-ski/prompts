title: Negative Constraints
level: Beginner
tags: style basics
description: State what to exclude, and pair every prohibition with what the model should do instead.
---

Often you know first what you do not want. A bare prohibition has two weaknesses, though: it does not say what should take its place, and it names the very thing you want to avoid. Models follow positive instructions more reliably than prohibitions, so a "don't" works best when it comes with what you want instead.

## Example

```prompt
Create a three-day itinerary for Paris.

- No museums: plan walks through neighborhoods, markets and parks instead.
- Don't go up the Eiffel Tower: if you include a view, choose a lesser-known viewpoint.
- No more than 50 euros a day for admission and food: give the approximate cost of each item.
- No typical tourist restaurants: suggest places where mostly locals eat, and say how to recognize them.
```

## When it helps

When a model keeps producing the same obvious thing, or when hard limits apply, such as budget, length or content that is off-limits. A prohibition without an alternative often just leads to the next most obvious substitute, or gets overlooked. For style, the positive form alone is often enough: "Write in short, factual sentences" works better than a list of banned phrases.
