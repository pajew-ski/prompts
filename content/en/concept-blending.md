---
title: Concept Blending
level: Intermediate
tags: creativity ideas
description: Combine two unrelated concepts into several new ideas and check whom each one serves.
---

Two concepts that have little to do with each other are combined into something new. A single blend usually lands on the most obvious combination. It gets more interesting when you ask for several variants that take different features from each concept. Asking what problem each variant solves, and for whom, separates usable ideas from mere curiosities.

## Example

```prompt
Concept A: [e.g. coffee maker]
Concept B: [e.g. alarm clock]

Combine the two concepts into three new product ideas. The three should differ in which features they take from A and which from B.

For each idea:
1. Name and a two-sentence description.
2. Which features come from A, which from B?
3. What problem does it solve, and for whom?
4. What is its biggest weakness?
```

## When it helps

Brainstorming for products, formats or services once the obvious ideas are used up. The feature breakdown shows which combinations have not been tried yet, so you can ask for another round on purpose. Limits: the results are starting points, not validated concepts. Whether the need really exists is something the model cannot tell you reliably.
