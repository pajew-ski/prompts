title: Counterfactual Reasoning
level: Advanced
tags: analysis reasoning

---

Counterfactual reasoning asks: what would have happened if a particular event had gone differently? The question becomes useful when the model does not just tell a story but builds a causal chain, states which factors stay the same, and names the point at which the alternative history converges with the real one again. That last point shows which developments hinged on the one event and which would have come anyway.

## Example

```prompt
Suppose the assassination attempt in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 had failed and Archduke Franz Ferdinand had survived.

1. Causal chain: describe step by step what would have developed differently in the following weeks and months. Justify each step.
2. Unchanged factors: which conditions would still have been in place, such as alliances, the arms race, nationalist tensions?
3. Convergence: at what point, if any, would the alternative course have come back in line with what actually happened? What speaks for and against that?
4. Uncertainty: which steps are well supported, and which are speculation?
```

## When it helps

Historical questions, but also decisions in hindsight, such as a product launch or a hiring decision. Asking for unchanged factors separates causes from mere triggers. Limits: the result is reasoned speculation, not knowledge. Check whether each step is plausible rather than following the story as a whole, since a fluently told chain sounds more convincing than it is.
