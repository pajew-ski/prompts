---
title: Flipped Interaction
level: Beginner
tags: dialogue questions
description: The model interviews you one question at a time and produces the result itself once it knows enough.
---

Usually you ask and the model answers. Here the roles are reversed: the model asks you one question at a time in order to build something for you, such as a text, a plan or an outline. It decides when it has enough and then delivers the result.

Each question builds on your previous answer. That helps when you are not yet sure what you want and only sort out your thoughts while answering.

## Example

```prompt
I want to write a blog post about [topic], but I don't have a clear idea for it yet.

Interview me. Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer before asking the next. Base each question on what you have learned so far. Along the way, find out who the audience is, what my own experience with the topic is, and what readers should do after reading.

When you know enough, say so and create an outline with a working title and three to five sections. Stop after ten questions at the latest.
```

## When it helps

For tasks where the material is in your head but not yet in order: job applications, concepts, speeches, personal plans. If you already know what you want, a direct request is faster. The question limit keeps the interview from running on indefinitely. If you only need a few clarifying questions before a well-defined task, a strategy where the model asks all its questions at once fits better.
