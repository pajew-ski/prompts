description: Collect questions instead of answers about a plan, systematically: who, what, where, when, why, how.
---

Star-bursting is a brainstorming method in which you collect questions rather than solutions. A six-pointed star stands for the six question words: who, what, where, when, why and how. Each point generates questions the plan has to answer. This exposes gaps before you commit to a solution.

## Example

```prompt
Plan: [Your plan, e.g. Our bakery with three shops wants to offer a delivery service for business customers.]

Do not propose any solutions yet. Instead, for each of the six question words, write five questions we need to answer before we launch:
- Who?
- What?
- Where?
- When?
- Why?
- How?

Then mark the five questions whose answers would change the plan the most, and say in one sentence for each why.
```

## When it helps

At the start of a project, a product or a decision, when it is not yet clear what needs to be settled. Picking the five most important questions is the most useful part: thirty questions are a list, five are a work plan. The model knows your situation only from your description, so the more context you give, the less generic the questions. Once the scope of a plan is fixed, the method is too broad.
